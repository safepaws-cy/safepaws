I · Endemic Stronghold

The Bonelli's Eagle

Aquila fasciata

A Mediterranean species; in Cyprus, the apex of a vertical world. Forty to fifty pairs hold the island — among the densest populations in the eastern basin. The cliff is its emblem; the Turkish pine is its actual home.

The apexTurn ↻

Of its life

  • 40–50 breeding pairs documented on Cyprus — among the densest in the eastern Mediterranean.
  • 70% of nests in Turkish pine (Pinus brutia); mean elevation 625 m. Only 18% on cliffs — subverting the textbook image.
  • Pair fidelity is high; territories pass down through generations of birds.
  • Threats: electrocution on overhead lines, illegal shooting, secondary poisoning.
Iezekiel et al. 2024 · IJEE · BirdLife Cyprus
II · The Cliff Falcon

Eleonora's Falcon

Falco eleonorae

A Mediterranean breeder that times its young to the autumn passage of songbirds. Three colonies hold the Cyprus south coast — Akrotiri, Episkopi, Cape Aspro at Petra tou Romiou — together among the species' eastern strongholds.

The autumn breederTurn ↻

Of its life

  • ~138 ± 8 breeding pairs across the three Cyprus colonies (6-year boat survey, 2012–2017).
  • Breeding success: 1.54 ± 0.85 fledglings per pair.
  • Episkopi pairs raise fewer fledglings than Akrotiri's — human disturbance the suspect.
  • Akrotiri sits within the British Sovereign Base Area — a protective accident of geography.
Hadjikyriakou et al. 2020 · Ornithological Applications · UKOTCF
III · The Migrant

The Honey Buzzard

Pernis apivorus

It does not breed here. Twice a year — north in spring, south in autumn — it threads the eastern Mediterranean's flyways. Cape Greco is a Cyprus bottleneck, where the bird passes within range of a gun.

The bottleneckTurn ↻

Of its passage

  • Cape Greco — a recognised migratory bottleneck for honey buzzards, red-footed falcons, pallid harriers, kestrels.
  • Historically a target of illegal spring shooting across the eastern Mediterranean, including Cyprus.
  • Specialist diet: wasp and bee larvae dug from underground nests — a strikingly narrow striving for a bird of its size.
  • EU Birds Directive Annex I; Bern Convention Appendix II.
BirdLife Cyprus · Cape Greco IBA
IV · A Program of Recognition

LIFE Bonelli eastMed

Game and Fauna Service · BirdLife Cyprus · HOS

A seven-year EU LIFE programme (2018–2025) coordinated across Greece and Cyprus. The Game and Fauna Service and BirdLife Cyprus secured €150,000 specifically to mark and insulate the wires that have been killing eagles.

The flagshipTurn ↻

The work

  • Total budget €4,232,584; EU contribution €3,174,403.
  • Satellite tracking revealed overhead lines as a leading mortality factor — 2nd for the Griffon Vulture, 3rd for the Bonelli's Eagle in Cyprus.
  • EMBoNet — the East-Med Bonelli's Eagle Network — coordinates monitoring across borders.
  • BirdLife Cyprus flagged significant delays in EAC's implementation of agreed line-marking. The recognition is not yet enacted.
LIFE17 NAT/GR/000514 · BirdLife Cyprus · Cyprus Mail 2025
V · The Model Elsewhere

The Messina Camps

CABS · Italy · Sicily–Calabria

Where Sicily nearly meets Calabria, the honey buzzard's spring passage was once met by guns. Since the 1980s, mixed teams of volunteers, ornithologists and police have stood under that sky each season.

The originTurn ↻

Outcomes

  • Honey-buzzard casualties at the strait fell from ~5,000 per year to roughly 100.
  • Estimated 85,000+ honey buzzards spared over three decades.
  • The camp model — visibility, patrol, witness — has been replicated across the central and eastern Mediterranean.
  • Cyprus's autumn anti-poaching work draws on the same playbook.
CABS · BirdLife International · BirdLife Cyprus
VI · The Everyday Resident

The Long-legged Buzzard

Buteo rufinus

The buzzard you actually see — over Mesaoria's stubble in summer, hunched on a pylon outside Larnaca in winter. Resident, breeding, comparatively common. The base note against which the rarer notes are heard.

The base noteTurn ↻

Of its life

  • Resident breeder across Cyprus's open and semi-open landscapes.
  • Diet dominated by rodents and reptiles — the same prey base targeted by the rodenticides that the barn-owl program now reduces.
  • A reminder that "everyday" species carry the bulk of ecological work — their persistence is the field on which the rare species depend.
  • Vulnerable to electrocution where it perches on uninsulated pylons.
BirdLife Cyprus — species accounts
A counter-articulation

The Wire

the line that kills

Cyprus's electricity grid carries a second cargo. Overhead lines and uninsulated pylons are now the second leading cause of death for the Griffon Vulture, and the third for the Bonelli's Eagle, on the island.

The pressureTurn ↻

The picture

  • LIFE Bonelli eastMed secured €150,000 in 2020 to mark dangerous spans; BirdLife Cyprus reports the work has been substantially delayed.
  • March 2025: another rare vulture was found electrocuted. BirdLife formally called on the Electricity Authority to act.
  • Illegal shooting and poisoning persist alongside — the island's wider raptor mortality is a stacked harm.
  • The technology to prevent these deaths exists. The instrument that delays it is administrative.
Vulture Conservation Foundation · Cyprus Mail · BirdLife Cyprus

VII

The Ethical Consequence

Each hawk is a magnificent creature, manifesting its striving in broad daylight — and a striving more easily punished for its visibility. The work of Safe Paws Cyprus — and of the programmes gathered above — is the recognition of that striving against the wire, against the gun, against the slow administrative weather that delays the protections we have already agreed.

The bestiary is not a list of curiosities. It is an accounting of what we are asked to keep alive.
