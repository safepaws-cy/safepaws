I · Endemic to Cyprus

The Scops Owl

Otus cyprius

Recognised as a full species only in the last decade. A double-noted song; darker plumage; partly resident. Its population trend is unknown — its persistence quietly demanding our attention.

The endemicTurn ↻

Of its life

  • Readily breeds in artificial nest-boxes (occupancy 5–11%) across forest, ecotone & village.
  • Its diet is principally insects (Orthoptera, Coleoptera), with mice, geckos and the Cyprus spiny mouse adding biomass.
  • First documented bat predation (Pipistrellus) on Cyprus, 2024.
  • Threatened by the felling of old trees and renovation of village houses — its cavities lost.
Iezekiel 2021 · Kassinis 2024 · Flint 2015
II · A Program of Recognition

The Cyprus Barn Owl

Tyto alba — BirdLife Cyprus & the Game and Fauna Service

A national-scale alliance between farmers and the owl. The recognition of an animal whose striving relieves the field of poison.

The flagshipTurn ↻

Outcomes

  • ≈1,328 nest-boxes installed across the island.
  • −58% rodenticide use (multinational review); −52% since 2021 by BirdLife Cyprus's count.
  • Rodenticides phased out entirely in Paliometocho, Kampia, Kampi tou Farmaka and Mammari.
  • National Action Plan presented to the Minister of Agriculture, June 2020.
BirdLife Cyprus · Bontzorlos 2024 · Cyprus Mail 2025
III · The Origin

The Mediterranean Initiative

Yossi Leshem · Tel Aviv · Sde Eliyahu

Where this work began, in 1981. A pilot in the Hula Valley grew into a basin-wide practice — owls without borders, recognised in Israel, Jordan, the Palestinian Authority.

The originTurn ↻

Scale & results

  • ~5,000 nest-boxes in Israel; ~400 in Jordan; ~100 in the West Bank; ~50 in Morocco.
  • Beit She'an valley: 86.7% of boxes used at least once (5-yr study); 85.2% breeding success.
  • −45% pesticide use attributed to the program.
  • Farmers buy and install boxes themselves — belief enacted in practice.
Peleg 2018 · Meyrom 2009 · Times of Israel
IV · Across the Aegean

Thessaly's Plains

TYTO · Tyto alba

Greece's modern echo of the model. From 100 boxes in 2019 to nearly 300 by 2023, set across agricultural plains where mice and barley share a field.

GreeceTurn ↻

Progress

  • ~60% mean occupancy; ~30% successful broods per year.
  • 700+ owlets fledged in deployed boxes.
  • Companion projects on Lemnos via MedINA.
TYTO Project · MedINA
V · The Iberian

The Eagle Owl

Bubo bubo

Europe's largest. Iberia is its stronghold — recovered from a century of persecution, still falling to electrified pylons and the silence of vanished rabbits.

Spain · Top predatorTurn ↻

Conservation

  • ~2,500–3,000 territories across Spain.
  • Dominant mortality: electrocution + decline of European rabbit (Oryctolagus cuniculus).
  • Endesa and SEO/BirdLife coordinate utility-line and species-level recovery work.
  • Genetics show a 20th-century bottleneck and subsequent expansion.
Endesa · PLOS ONE 2015
VI · The Farmland

The Little Owl

Athene noctua

A bird of dry-stone walls and old olives. One of the steepest farmland declines in 60 years of European data — its persistence tied to mosaic landscapes.

Italy · Farmland indicatorTurn ↻

What works

  • Hedgerows, dry-stone walls, traditional rural buildings — the architecture of cavity supply.
  • Nest-boxes proven to boost local populations where natural cavities are scarce.
  • Reproduction tied to landscape heterogeneity.
Bird Conservation Int. · Cambridge — Little Owl monograph
A counter-articulation

The Limestick

collateral cruelty

In Cyprus, an estimated 620,000 songbirds were illegally killed in autumn 2024 alone. The owl is taken as collateral — a striving caught and stilled.

The pressureTurn ↻

The picture

  • Trapping up ~90% on prior years (BirdLife Cyprus surveys, 2024).
  • Barn owls and other raptors regularly trapped on limesticks; documented Larnaca rescues.
  • Enforcement gaps cited as the primary driver of resurgence.
Cyprus Mail · BirdLife Cyprus — Combating Bird Crime

A Genealogy

Stops along the way
1981 — 1983
Israel pilots barn-owl biocontrol in the Hula Valley, then at Kibbutz Sde Eliyahu (Y. Leshem and partners).
2007
Beit She'an valley reaches ~300 boxes — >85% occupancy at least once over five years.
2015
The Cyprus Scops Owl is reclassified as a full species (Flint et al., Zootaxa).
2019
TYTO launches the Thessaly Barn Owl Project in Greece with 100 boxes.
2020
Cyprus's National Action Plan for the Barn Owl is presented to the Minister of Agriculture.
2021
First nest-box study confirms the endemic Cyprus Scops Owl breeds in artificial cavities.
2024
Multinational review: Israel −45%, Cyprus −58% pesticide use; the model expands to Morocco, Spain, Malaysia.
2025
Cyprus reports complete rodenticide phase-out in several communities.

VII

The Ethical Consequence

Each owl is a small striving in the night. The work of Safe Paws Cyprus — and of the partners gathered above — is the practical recognition of that fact: the protection of the other's persistence against poison, against limestick, against the loss of the cavity it has always known.

The bestiary is not a list of curiosities. It is an accounting of what we are asked to keep alive.
