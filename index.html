Baruch Spinoza

1632 — 1677 · AMSTERDAM · THE HAGUE

conatus essendi — striving to persevere in being

Unaquaeque res, quantum in se est, in suo esse perseverare conatur. — Each thing, insofar as it is in itself, strives to persevere in its being.

Ethica III, Propositio 6 (1677)

For Spinoza, this striving is not a property added to a thing — it is the thing's actual essence (essentia actualis). Referred to mind alone it is Will; referred to mind and body together, Appetite; when accompanied by consciousness of itself, Desire (cupiditas). Joy and sadness are not states but transitions of conatus toward greater or lesser perfection.
