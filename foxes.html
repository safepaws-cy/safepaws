A Bestiary of Striving

Foxes of Cyprus

One island carnivore, articulated six ways — and the road, the poison, the decree that hold its persistence in question.

Each Cyprus dog, each Egyptian vulture, each owl is a conatus. So too is the fox: the island's only wild carnivore, a single striving that has hunted these hills since before the first village rose — and is most easily condemned for the company it is wrongly said to keep. — after Stage VII, The Conatus

I · Endemic to Cyprus

The Cyprus Fox

Vulpes vulpes indutus

Described as a distinct island subspecies in 1907, and found nowhere else on Earth. With no wolves, lynx or bears beside it, it is the sole wild carnivore of the whole island — shaped by isolation into a lighter, smaller form.

The endemicTurn ↻

Of its life

  • Formally described by the Smithsonian naturalist Gerrit S. Miller in 1907; an endemic subspecies of the red fox.
  • The only wild carnivorous land mammal native to Cyprus — no wolves, no lynx, no bears.
  • Part of the island's long story of isolation, which once produced dwarf elephants and hippos.
  • Found across the whole island — Troodos pine, the Akamas, the Limassol vineyards, the edge of Nicosia.
Miller 1907 · iNaturalist · Biodiversity of Cyprus
II · Hunter & Gardener

The Pest-Controller

an omnivore's quiet service

Rodents, hares, reptiles, insects, eggs — and fruit and seed. By holding down rats and mice it is free pest control for the farmer; by scattering seed it quietly replants the island's native flora.

The benefactorTurn ↻

Of its diet

  • Omnivorous: rodents, rabbits and hares, birds, reptiles, insects, eggs, carrion, fruit and seed.
  • Curbs rodent numbers — protecting crops and reducing the diseases rodents carry.
  • Disperses native seeds through its droppings; an accidental gardener.
  • Studies elsewhere show the fox diet is largely rats — a problem species in Cyprus — yet no local study has measured the benefit.
Cyprus Island · BirdLife Cyprus · Cyprus Discovery
III · A Contested Recognition

The Decree

Game and Fauna Service · BirdLife Cyprus

The fox is no longer a quarry species — yet a 2016 decree still permits its "management and control." BirdLife Cyprus opposed it as unscientific: the recognition of the fox remains unfinished.

The disputeTurn ↻

The picture

  • April 2016: the Minister of the Interior issues a decree for fox "management and control" — traps and shooting with a game-warden escort.
  • BirdLife Cyprus objected: no scientific study of fox numbers or impact underpinned it; the scope was broad and open-ended.
  • Foxes eating hares or chukar is natural ecosystem function — not proof of harm.
  • BirdLife's prescription: healthy habitats, anti-poaching, and farm enclosures — not predator control.
BirdLife Cyprus 2016 · Game and Fauna Service
IV · The Cunning One

The Aloupou

the fox in Cypriot folklore

In Cypriot story the fox is the trickster — the aloupou who weighs the halloumi and eats the difference. Yet the island's own proverbs already knew the verdict was unfair.

The reputationTurn ↻

Of its name

  • In Greek the fox is the alepoú; in Cypriot dialect, the aloupou — a byword for cleverness and sly deception.
  • The folktale: asked to divide two children's halloumi fairly, the fox "balances" the scales by eating from the heavier side until nothing is left.
  • Proverbs: "Don't let the fox guard the chickens"; "The fox sleeps and dreams of roosters."
  • And the counter-proverb: "The fox has the name, but someone else is eating the chickens."
Heartland of Legends · Cyprus Discovery
V · The Stacked Harm

The Poison

rodenticides & illegal bait

The fox that eats poisoned rats is poisoned in turn. Cyprus leads the region in illegal poisoned-bait incidents — a harm the island shares with foxes across the Mediterranean.

The secondary killTurn ↻

The picture

  • Secondary poisoning: rodenticides move up the food chain into the fox that controls the rodents.
  • Cyprus records among the region's highest rates of illegal poisoned-bait use, with dozens of incidents and deaths reported in a single year.
  • The same anticoagulant poisons are documented in foxes internationally — a shared Mediterranean and global pattern.
  • The barn-owl nest-box programme, which cuts rodenticide use, indirectly protects the fox too.
Cyprus Mail 2024 · regional poisoning reviews
VI · The Night Neighbour

The Resident

a life lived after dusk

Mostly nocturnal, shy of people, faithful to one mate — the fox raises three to six spring cubs in hidden dens. The animal you rarely see, keeping the island in balance while it sleeps by day.

The everydayTurn ↻

Of its life

  • Chiefly nocturnal, with an instinctive distrust of people; rarely seen by day.
  • Mates in winter; a litter of about 3–6 cubs is born in spring, blind and helpless.
  • Strong pair bonds — both parents tend the young, and the fox tends to keep one mate.
  • Highly adaptable: dens in caves, dense scrub, or self-dug burrows with several exits.
Cyprus Island · Cyprus Discovery
A counter-articulation

The Road

the everyday killer

Long after the hunters were called off, the deadliest force the fox meets is the highway. Over a decade of Cyprus roadkill records, the fox was the single most-struck wild animal.

The pressureTurn ↻

The picture

  • In a ten-year study (2013–2022) of 27 main Cypriot roads, foxes were 44% of 1,985 recorded roadkills — the most-hit species.
  • Incidents concentrated on highways and peaked in spring and summer, around June.
  • Persecution still lingers: branded a poultry pest, the fox was hunted and poisoned heavily through 1970–1990.
  • Habitat fragmentation and urbanisation compound the toll — a stacked, mostly invisible harm.
PLOS ONE 2024 (roadkill study) · Cyprus Island

A Genealogy

Stops along the way
1907
Gerrit S. Miller describes the Cyprus fox as a distinct endemic subspecies, Vulpes vulpes indutus.
1970 — 1990
Branded a livestock and poultry pest, the fox is hunted and poisoned heavily; its numbers fall sharply.
2004
The fox is honoured on a Cypriot postage stamp — even as control policies continue.
2016
A decree permits "management and control" of the fox; BirdLife Cyprus objects to its lack of scientific basis.
2013 — 2022
A decade-long roadkill survey finds the fox the most-struck wild animal on Cyprus's main roads — 44% of cases.
2023
Photographs of dead foxes spark public outcry; calls grow for an independent study of the population.

VII

The Ethical Consequence

The fox is the island's last wild hunter — a single striving that asks little of us but our restraint. The work of Safe Paws Cyprus is the recognition of that striving against the road, against the poisoned bait, against the old reflex to blame the fox for another's crime.

The bestiary is not a list of curiosities. It is an accounting of what we are asked to keep alive.
